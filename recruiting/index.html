Your cap is reason number one, in your favor

Compensation tops every list. A $10K cap against the flat-fee shops is the thing to lead with, in a sentence an agent can repeat to a friend. The research says don't open with split mechanics in a first conversation, but make sure they know the number before the second one.

Who's most likely to move next

Agents who switched in the last year are 1.7 times more likely to switch again. Agents at an office that's lost more than 20% of its people to rivals are 1.9 times more likely to leave too. Three or four warning signs together, 1.8 times. That's a targeting list, not a guess.

What this means for a one-person brokerage

You're the product. "Most switching agents are hiring a better boss," in the words of the Recruiting Insight guide. That's your best pitch and your hardest limit at the same time: every agent you add is more of your phone. That's the number we're already tracking.

The one they named for Jeffery's story

Agents want new tools only if they're seamless. "Tech overload" came up as a pain point in the interviews. Same thing Jeffery said about Salesforce on Thursday: nobody logs in until it shows them something they care about.

4 · What the recruiting actually looks like

A named list, a slow cadence, and the broker on the phone

From Recruiting Insight's 2026 guide, built on 184,000-plus agents' closing records. The brokerages that hire 12 to 24 experienced agents a year all run the same machine.

The list100 to 300 namesLocal, from MLS closings. Not memory, not just referrals.
The sweet spot$4M to $8M a yearProductive from day one, not so big they want a deal you can't give.
The cadenceEvery 30 to 60 daysAn article, a check-in call, a market briefing. Not a pitch.
The rhythmWeekly blockMonthly review, quarterly pipeline audit.

First conversation

Discovery, not a pitch. How'd you get into this, what would you change, who do you talk to about big decisions. The candidate talks 80% of the time. Split mechanics wait for conversation two.

After the conversation

Handwritten note within 48 hours. Something useful every 30 days. A quarterly call with no agenda but listening. Notice their closings and milestones out loud. Hires happen when their situation changes, so you're still in touch when it does.

What kills it

Nearly 40% of agents ignore every cold recruiting email. Pushy or automated outreach was called a dealbreaker outright. Pitching too early, arguing with objections, and recruiting by mood instead of by calendar are the three mistakes the guide names most.

What agents will respond to

About three in four would answer a recruiter offering better compensation. More than half would listen to one with more leads or a clearer company vision. Compensation and direction, again.

5 · Agent refers agent

Your calculator is already pointed at the best channel

A referral from one of your agents turns a cold call into a 30-minute conversation. Agents refer people who work the way they do. Three ways brokerages pay for it, from a broker who runs one.

Finder's bonus at signing

Paid when the referred agent joins. Sized to their prior 12-month production, not a flat amount, so a $6M producer is worth more than a rookie.

Share of company dollar for a set period

After the referred agent's first closing, the referrer gets a percentage of the brokerage's cut, for a defined window. Ties the bonus to production that actually happened.

Anniversary bonus

Paid on the referred agent's one-year mark. Rewards the referral that stuck, and gives the referrer one more reason to stay too.

Where this meets the store

A signed referral is objective, so it's a clean store trigger if you want one. Suggestion, not a plan: a one-time credit at signing, and the cash bonus stays cash. The ERA experienced-agent bonus your calculator computes is the finder's version of this, already built. The amounts are yours.
